# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Project Overview

Tripp Horbinski's personal site (https://tripp.horbin.ski; `SITE_URL` in `src/lib/site.ts`): one page, opening on an interactive canvas scene of the Flatirons from Chautauqua Meadow, then his work by the numbers round Leonardo's Vitruvian Man in his AWS Golden Jacket, the projects behind those numbers, where he's worked, what he builds on his own time, and how to reach him. Built with Next.js 16, React 19 and Tailwind CSS 3. It's written for the people hiring him: it says what he does in his own words first, gets them to his resume in one click, leads with the impact of his work rather than the stops along the way, and is memorable after that.

## Commands

- `npm run dev` — Start dev server
- `npm run build` — Build static export (outputs to `out/`)
- `npm run lint` — Run ESLint
- `npm run resume:pdf` — Print the homepage, which on paper is just his resume, to `public/tripp-horbinski-resume.pdf` (needs the dev server running); run it whenever `src/content/career.ts` changes
- `npm run meadow:og` — Retake the link-preview image (see SEO)
- The meadow's and the figures' build scripts, the Vitruvian Man's among them (see Homepage and Figures)

No test framework is configured.

## Deployment

Static export deployed to GitHub Pages via GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/nextjs.yml`). Pushes to `main` trigger automatic deployment. The site uses `output: 'export'` with `trailingSlash: true` and unoptimized images (required for static hosting).

## Architecture

### One page

`/` (`src/app/page.tsx`) is the whole site: `Meadow` (`src/components/Meadow.tsx`, the opening screen), `Work` (`src/components/Work.tsx`, the page under it), `Resume` (`src/components/Resume.tsx`, print only) and his structured data. Besides it there's only the 404 (`src/app/not-found.tsx`, a way back to the meadow), `robots.ts` and `sitemap.ts`. The site used to have more pages (Career, Projects, Backpacking with an Arctic Circle Trail trip map, Stack, About, Writing, and contour maps of real places heading them); they were taken out to keep it one page, and they're in git history at commit `5848bad` if they're ever wanted back. So is the homepage's earlier career walk, cards held on the meadow's grass while the scroll ran the hour from dawn to night (commit `98d361a`), which gave way to leading with his numbers.

### Content

- `src/content/career.ts` — The one source for the homepage and its resume, and so the PDF, written with Tripp from his resume and an interview about his GoFundMe work: his recent years get the detail and his earlier roles their best one or two lines. A role can be told in `phases` (GoFundMe: cloud and platform 2023–24, then full stack 2025–26), each with its own dates and bullets. `WORK` is the homepage's selected work: the projects behind those bullets, newest first, in his voice and from the same facts (nothing the bullets don't say), each with its `impact` numbers; change a project and its bullet together. `CHAPTERS` picks which of those numbers, and the Golden Jacket's 13 certifications (its `impact` in `CERTIFICATIONS`), go round the Vitruvian Man and tells them as his career in two chapters, the way `intro` does: first, making software ship (2018–2024), then now, building what people use (2025–2026), each oldest first. `PROFILE.tagline` (his line under his name, first on the page: "Purveyor of fine software", like an old shop's sign), `intro` (leading the numbers) and `practice` (how he builds now, with Claude Code, leading his selected work) are in his voice; `summary` is the resume's
- `src/content/projects.ts` — What he builds on his own time
- `src/lib/site.ts` — `SITE_URL`, where to reach him (`EMAIL`, `LINKEDIN`, `GITHUB`, `CREDLY`), `RESUME_PDF` and `OG_IMAGE`

### Layout and styling

`RootLayout` (`src/app/layout.tsx`) gives a centered column (`max-w-xl mx-auto px-4`) that only the 404 uses; the homepage runs full width (`main:has(.home)`). An inline `<script>` in `<head>` sets the `dark` class on `<html>` from a stored `theme` or the system preference before paint, for the 404's colors; `<html>` uses `suppressHydrationWarning`. The meadow ignores the theme.

Tailwind CSS 3, configured with `darkMode: 'class'`. Design tokens are CSS custom properties (`--bg`, `--text-primary`, `--text-nav`, `--border`, `--accent`) with light and dark values in `globals.css`. Fonts: Josefin Sans (headings and labels), Lora (body) and JetBrains Mono, loaded via Google Fonts. Component styles are in `globals.css` `@layer components`; Tailwind drops any class there it can't find written out in the source, so components name such classes in full instead of building them from variables.

### Path Aliases

`@/*` maps to `./src/*` (configured in `tsconfig.json`).

### Homepage (`/`)

The scene is an interactive canvas of the Flatirons from Chautauqua Meadow (engine in `src/lib/meadow/`). The mountain is generated, not hand-drawn: `src/lib/meadow/terrain.ts` comes from USGS 3DEP lidar and NAIP imagery via the build-time scripts in `scripts/meadow/` (`npm run meadow:fetch` once to download into the gitignored `.cache`, then `npm run meadow:render`). Each rock/forest area carries its surface normal, so the engine lights it from the real sun position (`suncalc`) for any hour. The rolling meadow hills, the line where the near grass begins, and every tree between the meadow and the mountain come from the same data: trees are placed where the aerial imagery shows canopy, sized by how dense it is, and cast shadows from the real sun. `npm run meadow:trace -- photo.jpg` traces a photo into the same format, and `npm run meadow:calibrate` fits the lidar camera to a photo's skyline. Only trace photos you have rights to.

The meadow is the opening screen (`.home-hero`, a screen tall), with just his name, his line (`PROFILE.tagline`) and Resume (the PDF, in a new tab) over its sky (`.home-head`), and a chevron down to the numbers. Who he is and how to reach him are a scroll away, and clicking him in the meadow goes there too. The page under it scrolls it away like any page; out of sight it stops drawing (an `IntersectionObserver` calls the engine's `setVisible`). The scene opens at 2:45 PM, and the Options panel sets the hour, plays the day, turns on the sound, and opens the field guide.

Under it, on the paper the Vitruvian Man is printed on (`.page`, `#F3EAD6`), `Work.tsx` is a server component in five sections, two of them folded away (below): By the numbers (`#numbers`), his `intro` and then `CHAPTERS` round the Vitruvian Man in his Golden Jacket (`public/vitruvian.webp`, see Figures): the first chapter down the left, the second down the right, their headings level, so it reads left to right as before and now and down each side as time, each number saying where and when it's from and linking to its project (the Golden Jacket's, to Credly), and the figure captioned with the Golden Jacket and its Credly link; Selected work (`#work`), led by how he builds (`practice`), then each project in `WORK` with its dates, role and numbers beside its story; Experience (`#experience`), a line for each role, its phases, the Golden Jacket and his degree, and his resume to view or download; On my own time (`#projects`), a card each for `src/content/projects.ts`; and Get in touch (`#contact`), a little about him off the keyboard, then his email, LinkedIn, GitHub and Credly, each with its icon (`CONTACT`), and the PDF. Selected work and Experience are his career journey, folded away under the numbers for whoever wants more (`src/components/Journey.tsx`, the one client piece of the page): its button opens it, and a button at its end folds it again and goes back up to where it opened. It also opens for a link into it, a number's project or an address ending in one, scrolling there once it has, and for the browser's find-in-page (it's `hidden="until-found"` while folded, and opens on `beforematch`); folded, it's still in the page's HTML for search engines. Narrower, the chapters go under the figure side by side, and on a phone one after the other with two numbers to a row; a project's numbers go over its story, and the projects one to a row. Links between sections are plain anchors; `html` scrolls smoothly unless reduced motion is asked for.

Following the trail slides to the numbers. Tripp is a feature too: the engine picks him out by his traced outline (`onRunner`), and clicking him slides to getting in touch, with a keyboard-focusable link laid over him. A soft white glow pulses round him (`GLOW`, `GLOW_PERIOD` in `engine.ts`) so he reads as something to click: his silhouette blurred once per resize, drawn under him each frame at a breathing opacity, brightest while he's pointed at and outlined in white then, steady for reduced motion and dimmer at night (`haloOf`, `drawHalo`). His dogs stand with him (`DOGS` in `dogs.ts`, at `PRETZEL_SPOT` and `STELLA_SPOT` in `scene.ts`), both at his left hand and turned round to face him (`flip`): Pretzel, his Australian shepherd, on the trail a step nearer than him, nose to his knee, and Stella, his border collie, behind Pretzel, her head over Pretzel's back. Tall screens look as far right as it takes to keep him and his dogs in the picture (`RIGHT` in `engine.ts`); wider ones already do. They're a link to puppe.rs (`PUPPERS` in `site.ts`), his app for pet sitters, in a new tab: they glow the way he does, pointing at either lights and outlines both, with no label, and a point on a dog is neither Tripp nor the trail behind (`onDog`, whichever of a dog and him is nearer winning). A keyboard-focusable link is laid over the two of them, as over him (`onDogs`).

### Resume

`src/components/Resume.tsx` renders `career.ts` as his resume, hidden on screen (`.resume`). On paper the scene and the page under it (`.home`) drop out and it's all that prints, on Letter (the print styles in `globals.css`), so printing the homepage prints his resume, and `npm run resume:pdf` prints it to the PDF the page offers (`RESUME_PDF`), which leaves off the phone number his own PDF carries.

### Figures

The runner in the meadow is a screen print of Tripp traced from his 2024 Lakefront Marathon finish photo, drawn by `src/lib/meadow/runner.ts`: `npm run meadow:runner -- finish.jpg` cuts him out with Apple's Vision framework (`scripts/meadow/segment.swift`, macOS only), repairs what the photo hides (his friend's hand, his arm behind his friend, his feet below the frame, the top of his cap, his watch face), poses that free arm in a shaka (upper arm at his side, forearm opened out and down) as thick as his other arm, matches his shaded legs to his arms, and takes off the bib, medal and lanyard and reprints his La Sportiva singlet's design whole beneath them, all placed in that photo's pixels, then separates flat inks and traces them with potrace into the generated `src/lib/meadow/runner-art.ts`. His shoes are Hoka Clifton 11s, a simple 3D model projected onto his ankles. They print in tan (ATPS) with their inks tagged, and `drawRunner` takes the colorway: white in the meadow. He stands on the trail at `RUNNER_SPOT` (`scene.ts`), sized so his eyes sit on the horizon, with the grass in front of him drawn over him the way it is over the trail post.

A second figure, not on the site now, is traced from his photo crossing the Hoan Bridge mid-race: `npm run meadow:runner-bridge -- bridge.jpg` (`scripts/meadow/runner-bridge.mjs`) into the generated `src/lib/meadow/runner-bridge-art.ts` (`FIGURES` in `runner.ts` has both). So the two print alike, both scripts share everything but their photo's repairs through `scripts/meadow/screenprint.mjs`: the cutout, the painting tools, the singlet's design (drawn in the finish photo's pixels and carried onto the bridge torso by an affine fit), the Clifton model (posed in his stride with pitch), the Lab tone matching, and the ink separation, whose `detail` option sets how close to the photo a print comes (its defaults, `POSTER`, are the runner figures'). The bridge photo is shrunk to the finish photo's scale before separating, so both have the same detail. Its skin is matched to the finish figure's measured tones, and its moose tattoo is the finish figure's traced moose, read from `runner-art.ts` and carried onto his thigh, so regenerate the finish figure first. A change to `screenprint.mjs` should leave `runner-art.ts` byte-identical unless the finish figure is meant to change.

Stella, Tripp's border collie, is a third print, from his photo of her by the window one July afternoon: `npm run meadow:stella -- stella.jpg` (`scripts/meadow/stella.mjs`) into the generated `src/lib/meadow/stella-art.ts`, the same shape as `runner-art.ts` (its `shoes` go unused), and she stands in the meadow behind Pretzel (below). (An earlier print, from his photo of her in the grass one October morning, is in git history at commit `5b3018b`.) The phone keeps the photo sideways with a note to turn it, which Vision doesn't read, so the script turns it upright and shrinks it to 1500×2000 first, and everything is placed in those pixels. Vision's person mask isn't meant for a dog, so she's cut out by its foreground mask alone, and the sun behind her lights the tips of her coat as a bright rim, so her outline is shaved in 3 px (`SHAVE`). She's holding a tennis ball in the photo, which is taken out (`unball`): where it stands out past her neck her outline is cut back to the line her neck follows above and below it (`NECK`), her tongue's tip, which hangs behind it, is drawn on as a rounded end in the tongue's own color (`TIP`), and the rest is filled in from the fur around it but not from her tongue. The crease where her tongue's lobes fold together is too gray to tell from her fur by color and printed as a white speck, so inside her tongue (`TONGUE`) whatever isn't the dark of her mouth keeps the tongue's pink. A sunlit tuft standing up where her left ear meets her head printed as a bump on the ear, so it's cut back to a line from her head to her ear (`TUFT`). Her tones are shifted before separating (`retone`): her tongue keeps its pink; otherwise the room's warmth and the sky's blue are pulled most of the way back to neutral; her white is in shade, gray beside the sunlit carpet, so the lighter or warmer a tone the more it's lifted toward white; the sun's gray sheen on her black, duller than her sunlit white and cool where her shaded white is warm, is dimmed toward her black instead of printing as gray patches; and her near-black coat's deepest darks are spread apart so its form shows. She prints like Pretzel, in the runner figures' flat inks and thin outline (`POSTER`), from the photo shrunk to a quarter (`SCALE`), and her face and throat get their own inks inside a region whose edge runs through the black under her ears and down her neck. She's turned toward the camera, so her forepaw is lower in the photo than her hind paw, and her shadow in the meadow runs along that line (`stance` in `DOGS`).

Pretzel, Tripp's Australian shepherd, is a fourth print, and stands beside him in the meadow: from his photo of Pretzel on the landing of a wooden stair, `npm run meadow:pretzel -- pretzel.jpg` (`scripts/meadow/pretzel.mjs`) into the generated `src/lib/meadow/pretzel-art.ts`, the same shape as Stella's, and like hers drawn with `drawFigure` from `DOGS` (`src/lib/meadow/dogs.ts`). Pretzel prints like Tripp's figures (`POSTER`), in a few flat inks inside a thin outline to match the meadow's flat colors, its edge rounded off first so the outline doesn't trace every wisp of the coat (`ROUND`), from the photo shrunk to about a third (`SCALE`), so the print keeps to the detail the meadow can show and weighs less than Tripp's. Vision's foreground mask takes in the toy Santa standing behind, which is cut away along the rump where the fur over him thickens to the coat's gray (`SANTA`), his red showing through the wisps filled in from the gray around it; and it takes in the wisps at the edge of the coat, through which the wood shows. The copper points are too near the wood in color to pick those out by color alone, so each pixel within 10 px of the edge is weighed against the photo just outside and the coat just inside, and peeled off if it's nearer the outside (`peel`). The lamplight and the wood warm the whole coat and would print the blue merle brown, so tones near neutral are pulled back to it and the mid grays a little past it toward blue, while the copper points, more strongly colored, keep theirs, and in the shade it takes more color to count as copper (`retone`); the feathering under the chest and belly, tan with the floor through it, is pulled back however warm it is (`FEATHERS`). Each dog in `DOGS` is sized by the top of its shoulders in its photo (`withersY`) and how far up to Tripp's eyes that comes (`withers`): a bit over a quarter for Pretzel, about 17 inches at the shoulder, and a little more for Stella.

The Vitruvian Man heading the numbers is Leonardo's drawing (c. 1490, public domain) in Tripp's AWS Golden Jacket, hand-colored the way a print is: `npm run meadow:vitruvian -- vitruve.jpg` (`scripts/meadow/vitruvian.mjs`) into `public/vitruvian.webp`, from Luc Viatour's photograph of the drawing on Wikimedia Commons ("Da Vinci Vitruve Luc Viatour.jpg", 2258×3070, credited in the caption), with everything placed in its pixels. The ink is lifted off the paper by comparing the green channel, where the brown ink is darkest, with the paper round it (each pixel's brightest neighbor, after blurring away the paper's bright fibers), so the scan's uneven light and stains fall away and his hatching comes through lighter than his contours; specks left over are taken out with a small median. Only the ink inside the circle and the square is kept, and above the figure only the circle itself, followed along its own ink through Leonardo's writing (`arcOf`), so the writing goes. Only the man in front, arms out level, wears the jacket; the one behind, arms raised, stays as Leonardo drew him. Its sleeves are those level arms as Leonardo drew them (`ARMS`, the contours of all four traced by hand, with `sleeve` saying which are in the jacket), washed in gold under his lines, with ribbed cuffs at the wrists; its body is drawn over his chest, which is taken out under it, in lines roughened to match his (`BODY`, `COLLAR`, `BAND`, a zipper and a small "aws" over his heart). The jacket is all he wears, so his privates are taken out of the drawing below it (`GROIN`), the way a statue's are smoothed, with Leonardo's line across his hips drawn back through and his inner thighs meeting where they were. The jacket's shade is hatched in the drawing's ink and its colors are from Tripp's photo in it. It's printed on the page's paper (`PAPER`), which its section matches, since that compresses to about a third of a transparent print. sharp hands a one-channel image back as three after most operations, so the script keeps one with `extractChannel(0)`.

### SEO

- His `Person` structured data (JSON-LD) is on the homepage, from `src/lib/structured-data.ts`
- Sitemap (just `/`) and robots.txt generated by Next.js (`src/app/sitemap.ts`, `src/app/robots.ts`)
- The OpenGraph image is a screenshot of the homepage, `public/og.jpg` (1200×630, under 300 KB so every app shows it). Retake it with `npm run meadow:og` (`scripts/meadow/og.mjs`, headless Chrome against the dev server) when the scene changes. It's a real file with an extension on purpose: GitHub Pages types files by extension, and link previews skip an extensionless image served as `application/octet-stream`
- Every absolute URL (metadata, sitemap, robots, structured data) builds on `SITE_URL` in `src/lib/site.ts`, with the trailing slash pages are served at
